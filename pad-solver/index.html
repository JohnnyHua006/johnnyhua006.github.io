Quick start

  1. On the Edit tab, tap an orb colour then tap or drag across the board to draw the board you see in game. Or press Randomize to try it out.
  2. On the Team tab, pick a preset and press Load. Not sure which? Use Max combos — it just makes as many combos as possible.
  3. Press Solve. Results appear in a few seconds.
  4. Tap a result to draw its path on the board, then press ▶ to watch the orb move and the combos resolve. Open Step-by-step guide for the short written version, and Practice to try the drag yourself and be marked on it.

Nothing is uploaded and nothing is downloaded — the whole tool is this one file, running on your device.

What the tool is doing

In Puzzle & Dragons you pick up one orb and drag it, swapping with each orb you pass through, and nothing matches until you let go. So a solution is a starting orb plus a list of moves — which is exactly what the results give you.

The solver tries hundreds of thousands of possible drags and scores the board each one would leave behind, then shows you the best handful. It cannot try every possibility — there are more than there are atoms in the room — so think of it as a very strong suggestion, not a proof.

Reference

Which preset should I pick?

A preset is just a shortcut that fills in the Team and Objectives tabs for you. After loading one you can change anything you like.

Team tab: weights and awakenings
Weight
Roughly how many monsters on your team attack with that colour. One per monster, plus about 0.3 if it is their sub-colour. Set a colour to 0 and the solver stops caring about matching it for damage.
TPA
How many Two-Pronged Attack awakenings your team has in that colour. Each one multiplies damage by 1.5 when you match exactly 4 connected orbs.
VDP
Damage Void Piercer awakenings. Needs a 3x3 square of 9 orbs. The only way to hurt an enemy that voids damage.
Row
Enhanced Row awakenings. Each adds 20% when you fill a whole horizontal row with that colour.
L
L-Unlock awakenings, which need an L shape of 5 orbs.
7c (Enhanced Combo)
Doubles damage once you reach 7 combos, per awakening. Set this and the solver will fight hard to hit 7.
Leaders
Your leader and your friend's leader. Pick the archetype that matches what they need — a combo count, a number of colours, a row, a cross. Both multipliers apply at once.
Target enemy
Optional. Sets the 2x / 0.5x colour advantage. Fire beats wood beats water beats fire; light and dark beat each other.
Objectives tab: Required vs Bonus

Required means a solution that misses it is thrown out, no matter how much damage it does. Use it for things you truly must do this turn — sweep the poison, trigger a cleanse, hit a combo shield.

Required matches is the one to reach for when your team needs specific shapes. Add a row per shape — say 1× TPA light and 1× TPA water — and every solution offered will make all of them, with combos and damage maximised on top. Each row is judged on its own, so a board that manages one of the two still ranks above a board that manages neither; that is what lets the search work toward them instead of stumbling onto them. Expect to trade a little: on a test board, forcing those two TPAs cost one combo versus letting the solver run free — which it would not have made at all on its own.

Bonus means the solver prefers it but will trade it away for a better board.

If every option comes back with a red ✗ on a Required objective, it could not find any way to do it — try a longer time budget, more max steps, or allowing diagonals.

Reading the results
Max score
The best board it found, ignoring how long the path is.
Shortest path (98% of best)
The fewest moves that still gets you almost the same board. Usually the one to actually play — fewer moves means less chance of fumbling it and less time on the clock.
"93% of best in 25 steps"
A middle option: this much of the best score for that many moves. Nothing else found was both better and shorter.
The number on the right
The score. It only means something compared with the other options for the same team — it is not damage in millions.
"6 combos guaranteed"
Combos the board is certain to make, including cascades where falling orbs line up again. This never depends on luck.
"~6.8 with skyfall"
An average over random refills of what you might get once new orbs drop in. Informational only — it never changes the ranking unless you turn that on in Solver settings.
✓ / ✗ tags
Whether each objective was met.
Following and memorising a path

A 30-step drag drawn as one long snake is close to impossible to memorise, so the path is cut into numbered moves, each drawn in its own colour with a matching numbered badge where it starts. The board and the written guide use the same numbers and colours.

Open Step-by-step guide under the board for the short version — something like →3 ↓2, Loop clockwise, Staircase down/right. Repeated shapes are collapsed, so a loop walked twice reads as ×2 rather than eight separate arrows. Tap any move to spotlight just that piece on the board and jump playback to it.

Rewind puts the board back exactly as it was before you pressed play, keeping the solution selected, so you can study the starting board and the path together after watching it resolve. Hide path clears the drawing without giving up the solution — useful for reading the board on its own, or testing yourself before opening Practice. The board can only be edited while the Edit tab is open, so tapping it while reviewing a result will never repaint an orb.

▶ plays the drag. The held orb glides into place and the orb it passes glides back the other way, exactly as they do in game, and every other orb fades back so there is only one thing to follow. Drag the slider to scrub. Resolve skips to the end and plays the matches clearing, wave by wave. Exit puts your board back exactly as it was — playback never edits it.

Practice mode

Press Practice and the path disappears. Drag an orb across the board from memory, exactly as you would in game, and let go — the tool then tells you whether you built the board it was aiming for.

It marks the board, not your route. If you reach the same arrangement by a different drag that counts as correct, because it is worth exactly the same in game. Miss it and you are told how many orbs are out of place and how many combos your attempt actually makes, so you can see how close you were.

Try again resets the board, Show path brings the solution back to compare against, and Done leaves practice.

Solver settings
Engine
Beam explores many promising drags at once and is the better all-rounder. Iterative deepening checks all short paths before longer ones, so it is the one to try when you want the fewest possible moves.
Beam width
How many possibilities it keeps alive at once. This is the setting that most affects combo count. Lower is faster, higher finds more.
Time budget
How long to search. The solver is happy to be stopped early — Cancel still hands back the best path it has found.
Node budget
A safety cap on total work. Left high so the clock is what stops the search.
Max steps
The longest drag it may suggest. Long drags score better but are harder to execute and eat the move timer.
Shortest-path threshold
How much score you are willing to give up for a shorter path. At 98%, the short option must still score at least 98% of the best.
Penalty per step / per diagonal
How much a longer or fiddlier path counts against a solution when ordering the leftover options.
Allow diagonal moves
Diagonals are legal in game but hard to execute cleanly, so they are off by default.
Rank on guaranteed combos only
On by default, and worth leaving on: it stops the solver from recommending a board that only looks good if lucky orbs drop in.
Glossary
Combo
One group of 3+ matched orbs. Connected same-colour matches count as one combo, so a 2x3 block of red is one combo of six orbs, not two.
Cascade
After matches clear, the orbs above fall — and sometimes line up into another match on their own. Those are free and guaranteed.
Skyfall
The brand-new orbs that drop in from the top. Random, so this tool never counts on them.
TPA
A match of exactly 4 connected orbs.
VDP
A 3x3 square of 9 orbs; pierces damage void.
Row
A full horizontal line of one colour, all the way across.
Cross
A plus shape of 5 orbs.
Mass attack
Any match of 5+ orbs hits every enemy, not just one.

Board letters

R fire · B water · G wood · L light · D dark · H heart · J jammer · P poison · M mortal poison · o empty

Jammers and poison only match with their own kind. They deal no damage but still add to your combo count, and poison hurts you — which is why "clear all poison" exists as an objective.

About / notes

Run it: double-click index.html. No server, no build step, no network requests — it works offline from file:// and identically on GitHub Pages. Everything (markup, styles, engine, solver, worker) lives in this one file.

Board string: one letter per cell, row-major (left to right, top to bottom): R fire, B water, G wood, L light, D dark, H heart, J jammer, P poison, M mortal poison, o empty. A 6×5 board is 30 letters. If any orb carries a state, a tail is appended after a / with one character per cell: . none, + enhanced, L locked, * both. Boards without states encode to the plain format exactly.

Dawnglare: the Board text card also emits a pad.dawnglare.com link so a board can be cross-checked in the community simulator. Its legend uses the same letters we do for the eight orbs it knows (R B G L D H J P); it has no code for mortal poison and no empty cell, so those two degrade in that view and the app tells you when it happens. Our own string stays the source of truth.

Saved data: the last board, your team profile and the solver settings are kept in this browser via localStorage, under the single key pad-solver.v1. Nothing leaves your device.

Tuning the numbers: every damage and awakening constant sits in one CONFIG object in the score section — combo and match multipliers, TPA / VDP / row / 7c / L values, the attribute wheel, path costs and skyfall sample count. GungHo rebalances awakenings periodically, so treat those as editable rather than fixed.

Orb art is drawn procedurally as inline SVG, so no game sprites are bundled. To use your own local sprite sheet instead, edit the SPRITE object near the top of the render section (enabled, url, cols, rows, order) and point url at a file next to index.html or a data: URI.

Tests: open index.html?test or press the button below to run the inline suite — matching and the connected-merge rule, deterministic cascades, every shape detector, the scoring model, the solver, and the path guide.

Credits: inspired by padopt / pndopt / combo.tips for its profile-and-weights model and single-page approach, and by pazusoba for the fixed-size priority-queue beam search and its profile types. Both were read as references only; this implementation is independent. Dawnglare is the community-standard simulator used here as the interop target.
